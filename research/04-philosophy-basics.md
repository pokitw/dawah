# 04 — Philosophy Basics (Very Simple)

## 1) What is an argument?
An argument = reasons (premises) that lead to a point (conclusion). Example:
- P1: All humans die.
- P2: I am a human.
- C: I will die.

## 2) Valid vs Sound (very important)
- **Valid** = the SHAPE is right. If premises were true, the conclusion would HAVE to be true.
- **Sound** = valid AND the premises are actually TRUE.
- A valid argument can still be wrong if a premise is false. Always check: (a) Is the shape valid? (b) Are the premises true?

## 3) Common Fallacies (with debate examples)
- **Strawman** — twisting the other side. (Atheist: "You think God is a magic man in the sky." That's a strawman of tawhid.)
- **Ad hominem** — attacking the person, not the argument. ("You're just a brainwashed believer.")
- **God of the gaps** — "science can't explain X yet, so God." (Be careful; use only where the gap is deep, like consciousness.)
- **Genetic fallacy** — judging a belief by where it came from. ("You only believe because you were born Muslim.")
- **False dilemma** — offering two options when more exist. (Euthyphro is a false dilemma — there's a third option.)
- **Circular reasoning** — using the conclusion as a premise. ("The Quran is true because it says it's true.")
- **Composition fallacy** — thinking the whole must share a part's property. ("Each brick is light, so the wall is light.")
- **Is/ought fallacy** — jumping from facts ("is") to values ("ought") with no bridge. (Key against science-based morality.)
- **Appeal to authority (misused)** — "A famous scientist is atheist, so atheism is true." Fame ≠ proof.
- **Moving the goalposts** — after you meet their demand, they change the demand.

## 4) How to Steelman (do this always)
1. Repeat their argument in your own words.
2. Make it as STRONG and fair as you can.
3. Ask: "Did I get that right?"
4. THEN answer. (This builds trust and is better dawah — Quran 16:125.)

## 5) Socratic Method (asking good questions)
Instead of lecturing, ask gentle questions so the person thinks:
- "What do you mean exactly by 'proof'?"
- "Would anything ever change your mind? What would?"
- "If there's no objective right and wrong, was any historical atrocity REALLY wrong, or just disliked?"
- "Where do you think the universe came from?"
Good questions open hearts better than winning points.

## 6) Burden of Proof
Whoever makes a claim must support it. "There is no God" is also a claim (for a strong atheist). "I'm not convinced" (agnostic) is different. Ask them which one they mean.
