# 01 — Arguments For God (Islamic Side)

Each argument has: a simple explanation, the formal steps, sources, the best atheist objection, the best Islamic reply, and a difficulty level.

---

## ARG-1: The Quran's Own Argument (Quran 52:35)
**id:** arg-quran-5235 | **difficulty:** Easy

**Simple (like to a 14-year-old):** Look at yourself. You did not make yourself. And nothing makes itself out of nothing. So Someone made you. That Someone is Allah.

**Formal steps:**
- P1: We exist.
- P2: There are only three options: we came from nothing, OR we made ourselves, OR a Maker made us.
- P3: "From nothing" is impossible (nothing has no power to do anything).
- P4: "Made ourselves" is impossible (a thing must exist before it can act, so it can't create itself).
- C: Therefore a Maker (Allah) made us.

**Quran/Sunnah:** Quran 52:35–36 — "Were they created by nothing, or were they the creators [of themselves]? Or did they create the heavens and the earth?"

**Classical scholars:** Ibn Kathir (tafsir of 52:35), Ibn Uthaymin (explained in islamqa 26745).

**Modern presenters:** Hamza Tzortzis (*The Divine Reality*); Yaqeen ("The Case for Allah's Existence").

**Sources (URLs):**
- https://islamqa.info/en/answers/26745
- https://islamqa.info/en/answers/13532
- https://yaqeeninstitute.org/read/paper/the-case-for-allahs-existence-in-the-quran-and-sunnah

**Strongest atheist objection:** "This is a false trilemma. Modern physics says the universe may not need a cause in the normal sense; quantum events can look uncaused."

**Best Islamic reply:** The verse is about US and created things, which clearly begin. Even if the physics of the very beginning is debated, the deep point stands: dependent things cannot explain themselves. See ARG-2 and ARG-3.

## ARG-2: Kalam Cosmological Argument (al-Ghazali)
**id:** arg-kalam | **difficulty:** Medium

**Simple:** Everything that has a starting point was started by something. The universe had a starting point. So something started the universe.

**Formal steps:**
- P1: Whatever begins to exist has a cause.
- P2: The universe began to exist.
- C: The universe has a cause. (That cause is beginningless, immaterial, powerful — this points to God.)

**Support for P2:** (a) An endless backward chain of past events (infinite regress) is impossible; (b) science (Big Bang, expansion) points to a beginning.

**Classical scholars:** Abu Hamid al-Ghazali, *Tahafut al-Falasifa (The Incoherence of the Philosophers)* — al-Ghazali argued the universe must have a beginning and thus a cause. Earlier: al-Kindi.

**Modern presenters:** William Lane Craig revived it (non-Muslim); Muslim du'at use it widely.

**Sources:**
- https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/cosmological-argument/
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalam_cosmological_argument

**Strongest atheist objection:** "P1 is from everyday experience INSIDE the universe; we can't apply it to the universe as a whole. Also some models have an eternal or quantum-origin universe (Graham Oppy pushes this)."

**Best Islamic reply:** "Begins to exist → has a cause" is a very well-supported principle; the alternative (things pop into being from nothing) is worse. Eternal-universe models face the impossibility of a completed infinite past. Mark honestly: the science of t=0 is not fully settled, so lean on the philosophical point, do not overclaim.

**Weak version to AVOID:** Saying "science has PROVEN God with the Big Bang." Too strong. Say it is "consistent with" a beginning.

## ARG-3: Contingency Argument (Ibn Sina / Avicenna)
**id:** arg-contingency | **difficulty:** Hard

**Simple:** Some things could have not existed (you, trees, stars). They only exist because something else holds them up. This can't go on forever with no foundation. There must be One who exists by Himself and needs nothing. That is Allah — the Necessary Existent (al-Wajib al-Wujud).

**Formal steps:**
- P1: Contingent things exist (things that could have not existed). [Thequran](https://thequran.love/2025/01/23/avicennas-ontological-argument-for-the-existence-of-god-from-the-crown-verse/?claude-citation-11004a75-a140-4d89-8cd6-334ed2d70a0d=39be311f-0034-4eab-9287-718bafbeef72)
- P2: A contingent thing needs something else to explain why it exists.
- P3: The whole collection of contingent things is still contingent, so it too needs an explanation.
- P4: That explanation cannot itself be contingent (or the problem repeats).
- C: There must be a Necessary Being that exists by itself. That is God. [The Path of Mystics](https://pathofmystics.com/existence-of-god-avicenna/?claude-citation-11004a75-a140-4d89-8cd6-334ed2d70a0d=8d8e7604-8dc1-4dcc-be73-a1f63d303bb9)

**Classical scholar:** Ibn Sina (Avicenna), *Burhan al-Siddiqin (Proof of the Truthful)*, in al-Najat and al-Isharat. [Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/religious-studies/article/varieties-of-avicennian-arguments-for-the-existence-of-god/70F2B4828AA2E1977A71667025635CF2?claude-citation-11004a75-a140-4d89-8cd6-334ed2d70a0d=0b26f6c7-a244-472a-8782-b6749ce12321)

**Sources:**
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seddiqin_argument
- https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/religious-studies/article/varieties-of-avicennian-arguments-for-the-existence-of-god/70F2B4828AA2E1977A71667025635CF2

**Strongest atheist objection:** "This may commit the fallacy of composition — parts being contingent doesn't prove the whole is. Maybe the universe itself is the necessary, brute thing (Russell: 'the universe is just there')."

**Best Islamic reply:** The universe is made of changing, dependent parts, so it looks contingent, not necessary. [The Path of Mystics](https://pathofmystics.com/existence-of-god-avicenna/?claude-citation-11004a75-a140-4d89-8cd6-334ed2d70a0d=2ddb18df-5258-43de-b020-ca5277e24707) Calling it a "brute fact" just stops asking the question; the contingency argument keeps asking until it reaches a Being that truly needs nothing.

## ARG-4: The Fitrah Argument (Quran 30:30)
**id:** arg-fitrah | **difficulty:** Easy

**Simple:** Deep inside, people naturally feel there is a God, especially in hard moments. Islam says we are born with this feeling (fitrah).

**Quran:** 30:30 — "So direct your face toward the religion, inclining to truth. [Adhere to] the fitrah of Allah upon which He has created [all] people." [IslamAwakened](https://www.islamawakened.com/quran/30/30/?claude-citation-11004a75-a140-4d89-8cd6-334ed2d70a0d=7ab941d8-c3d4-438c-ac50-f551687dcb7f)

**Hadith:** "No child is born except upon the fitrah, then his parents make him a Jew, a Christian, or a Magian." — Sahih al-Bukhari 1358 (Book of Funerals; also appears at 1385 and in Tafsir at 4775) and Sahih Muslim 2658 (Book of Destiny). Grade: agreed upon (muttafaqun 'alayhi). Verified on sunnah.com.

**Classical scholars:** Ibn Taymiyyah wrote a lot on fitrah (Dar' Ta'arud al-'Aql wa'l-Naql).

**Sources:**
- https://islamqa.info/en/answers/248517
- https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1358
- https://sunnah.com/muslim:2658

**Strongest atheist objection:** "Feeling something does not make it true. Evolution could give us a 'God instinct' for survival, with no real God behind it (a debunking argument)."

**Best Islamic reply:** Fitrah is not the WHOLE proof — it is one sign among many. The intellectual arguments (ARG-1,2,3) plus the fitrah together are strong. And "evolution gave us the feeling" does not prove the feeling is false; it just gives a story of how we got it.

## ARG-5: The Moral Argument
**id:** arg-morality | **difficulty:** Medium

**Simple:** We all feel some things are REALLY wrong (torturing a child for fun) — not just "wrong for me." But if there is no God, where do these real, for-everyone rules come from? Islam says they come from God.

**Formal steps:**
- P1: If God does not exist, objective moral values do not exist.
- P2: Objective moral values DO exist.
- C: Therefore God exists.

**Key admission from an atheist:** J.L. Mackie (atheist philosopher), *The Miracle of Theism* (Oxford, 1982): "if there are objective values, they make the existence of a god more probable than it would have been without them. Thus we have a defensible argument from morality to the existence of a god." (Mackie escaped the conclusion by DENYING objective values exist — his "error theory.")

**Sources:**
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argument_from_morality
- https://www.patheos.com/blogs/secularoutpost/2014/10/17/j-l-mackies-argument-from-queerness-against-objective-values/

**Strongest atheist objection:** "We can ground morals in human wellbeing, empathy, and society (Sam Harris). [CrossExamined](https://crossexamined.org/can-atheism-account-for-objective-morality/?claude-citation-11004a75-a140-4d89-8cd6-334ed2d70a0d=23c7b844-eede-45e6-b1c2-10ca79ef4dcb) No God needed. Also Euthyphro: are things good because God says so (then it's arbitrary), or does God say so because they're good (then good is above God)?"

**Best Islamic reply:** Wellbeing-based ethics still can't jump from "is" to "ought" (the is/ought problem). [Sean McDowell](https://seanmcdowell.org/blog/can-atheism-explain-moral-oughtness?claude-citation-11004a75-a140-4d89-8cd6-334ed2d70a0d=bb4c572d-c307-4e82-b3f7-d41fb3d774cb) For Euthyphro, see OBJ-4: God's commands flow from His perfectly good NATURE — not arbitrary, and not a standard above Him.

## ARG-6: Consciousness (The Hard Problem)
**id:** arg-consciousness | **difficulty:** Hard

**Simple:** Science can explain how the brain processes signals. But it cannot explain WHY there is a "what it feels like" inside you (the taste of mango, the feeling of red). This inner experience is a mystery for pure matter-only views.

**Key source (non-Muslim):** David J. Chalmers named this "the hard problem of consciousness" in "Facing Up to the Problem of Consciousness," Journal of Consciousness Studies 2(3):200–219 (1995), and in *The Conscious Mind* (Oxford University Press, 1996).

**Modern Muslim presenter:** Hamza Tzortzis uses this in *The Divine Reality*.

**Sources:**
- https://iep.utm.edu/hard-problem-of-conciousness/
- https://consc.net/papers/facing.pdf

**Strongest atheist objection:** "God of the gaps — you point to a thing science hasn't explained YET and say 'God did it.' Science may solve consciousness later."

**Best Islamic reply:** This is not just an unsolved gap; many philosophers argue it is a gap of the WRONG KIND — you cannot get first-person experience out of third-person physical description in principle. Still, present it humbly as a strong pointer, not a knockout "proof."

**Weak version to AVOID:** "Science will NEVER explain anything about the brain." Too strong; only the felt, first-person part is the hard problem.

## ARG-7: Fine-Tuning / Design
**id:** arg-finetuning | **difficulty:** Medium

**Simple:** The numbers of the universe (like the force of gravity) are set in a super-narrow range that allows life. Change them a tiny bit and no life, no stars. This looks designed.

**Formal steps:**
- P1: The constants are fine-tuned for life.
- P2: This is either chance, necessity, or design.
- P3: Chance is wildly unlikely; necessity is unproven.
- C: Design (God) is the best explanation.

**Sources:**
- https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/fine-tuning/

**Strongest atheist objection:** "The MULTIVERSE: if there are countless universes with random settings, at least one will be life-friendly by chance — and of course we live in that one."

**Best Islamic reply:** The multiverse is not observed; it's an extra, unproven assumption to avoid a Designer. Even if a multiverse exists, a "universe-generator" itself would need setup. Present fine-tuning as a strong probability point, not a certainty.
