# 02 — Atheist Objections (Fair + Strong) With Islamic Answers

Rule: state the objection in its STRONGEST fair form first (steelman). Then answer. Mark which answers are strong vs weak.

---

## OBJ-1: Problem of Evil — Logical Form
**id:** obj-evil-logical | **who:** Epicurus (ancient), J.L. Mackie (modern) | **difficulty:** Medium

**Steelman:** A God who is all-good, all-powerful, and all-knowing would stop all evil. Evil exists. So such a God does not exist. (Mackie, "Evil and Omnipotence," 1955.)

**Islamic response:**
- The logical version is now widely seen as failing, even by many philosophers, because it assumes God can have NO good reason to allow any evil — which is not proven. (Alvin Plantinga's free-will defense broke this.)
- Islam: life is a TEST (Quran 67:2 — "[He] who created death and life to test you as to which of you is best in deed"). A test needs the possibility of hardship and wrong choices.
- Free will: real choice needs the option to do wrong.
- Limited human wisdom: we see a small piece; Allah sees all (see Yaqeen problem-of-evil paper).
- See all of Allah's names TOGETHER: Merciful, Wise, Just — plus perfect justice in the hereafter.

**Sources:**
- https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/evil/
- https://yaqeeninstitute.org/read/paper/the-problem-of-evil-a-multifaceted-islamic-solution

**Likely comeback:** "Free will explains human evil, but not disease, earthquakes, or child cancer."

**Notes:** The free-will defense is STRONG for man-made evil. For natural suffering, move to OBJ-3 and the "test + hereafter + limited wisdom" answers.

## OBJ-2: Problem of Evil — Evidential Form
**id:** obj-evil-evidential | **who:** William Rowe, Paul Draper | **difficulty:** Hard

**Steelman:** Maybe God COULD have reasons for some evil. But the huge amount of apparently pointless suffering makes God UNLIKELY (not impossible). Rowe's example: a fawn burning to death in a forest fire, unseen, helping no one. Draper: the pattern of pain and pleasure fits a "no-God" world better than a God world.

**Islamic response:**
- "Appears pointless to us" is not "is pointless." This is the key move (skeptical theism): our knowledge is tiny. Rowe's step from "we see no reason" to "there is no reason" is weak.
- Islam adds a positive story: suffering can raise ranks, erase sins, wake people up, and be perfectly compensated in the hereafter.
- Ibn al-Qayyim: Allah's allowing of evil serves greater goods and wisdoms (see Zeni's Yaqeen paper).

**Sources:**
- https://iep.utm.edu/evil-evi/
- https://yaqeeninstitute.org/read/paper/the-divine-wisdom-in-allowing-evil-to-exist-perspectives-from-ibn-al-qayyim

**Likely comeback:** "That's an unfalsifiable escape — you just say 'hidden reasons' for anything."

**Notes:** STRONG answer = skeptical theism + hereafter. WEAK answer = pretending we know the exact reason for each tragedy. Don't do that.

## OBJ-3: Animal and Infant Suffering (Hardest Case)
**id:** obj-animal-suffering | **difficulty:** Hard

**Steelman:** Animals and babies have no free will and no sin, yet they suffer terribly. Free-will and test answers seem not to apply.

**Islamic response:**
- Human free will still explains much (pollution, neglect, war harming children).
- Islam teaches justice for animals too; there are reports of settling of accounts among creatures on the Day of Judgment.
- A baby who dies is not "punished"; Islam has hope-filled positions about children.
- Limited wisdom + perfect afterlife compensation remain the backbone.

**Sources:**
- https://yaqeeninstitute.org/read/paper/why-do-people-suffer-gods-existence-the-problem-of-evil

**Notes:** Be gentle and honest. Say clearly: "This is one of the hardest questions; here is how Islam approaches it," not "this is easy."

## OBJ-4: Euthyphro Dilemma
**id:** obj-euthyphro | **who:** Plato (Socrates) | **difficulty:** Medium

**Steelman:** Is something good because God commands it, or does God command it because it is good? If the first, morality is arbitrary (God could command cruelty). If the second, goodness is above God, so we don't need God for morality. [A Level Philosophy \& Religious Studies](https://alevelphilosophyandreligion.com/euthyphro-dilemma-divine-command-theory/?claude-citation-11004a75-a140-4d89-8cd6-334ed2d70a0d=674b79a0-348b-4aa1-8ca1-6e2c0663145b)

**Islamic response (the "third option"):** This is a FALSE dilemma. God's commands flow from His own perfectly good NATURE. Goodness is not a random choice (not arbitrary), and not a standard outside/above God (not independent). It IS God's nature. So God cannot command cruelty for its own sake, because that would contradict who He is.

**Sources:**
- https://iep.utm.edu/divine-command-theory/

**Likely comeback:** "Then 'good' just means 'God's nature' — that's defining good into existence / circular."

**Notes:** STRONG answer = grounding in God's nature. This is the mainstream response used by Tzortzis and classical theists.

## OBJ-5: Divine Hiddenness
**id:** obj-hiddenness | **who:** J.L. Schellenberg | **difficulty:** Hard

**Steelman:** If a loving God wanted a relationship with us, He would make His existence obvious to every honest seeker. But there are sincere "non-resistant non-believers" who look and still don't find Him. So a perfectly loving God probably does not exist. (Schellenberg, *Divine Hiddenness and Human Reason*, Cornell University Press, 1993.)

**Islamic response:**
- God is NOT fully hidden: there are signs everywhere (fitrah, the universe, the Quran). The "proof" is available; the test is partly whether we humble ourselves to accept it.
- Faith is meant to be a test (Quran 67:2). Total, forced certainty would remove the test.
- Some "non-resistance" is not as pure as claimed — pride, desire, and environment block the heart.
- Guidance is from Allah; He knows who truly seeks (Quran 16:125 ending).

**Sources:**
- https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/divine-hiddenness/
- https://iep.utm.edu/divine-hiddenness-argument-against-gods-existence/

**Notes:** Answer with humility; don't accuse a specific person of secretly resisting. Talk about signs + the wisdom of a test.

## OBJ-6: Who Created God?
**id:** obj-who-created-god | **who:** Richard Dawkins ("who designed the designer?") | **difficulty:** Easy

**Steelman:** If everything needs a cause/designer, then God needs one too. If God can be uncaused, why not just say the universe is uncaused and skip God?

**Islamic response:**
- The principle is NOT "everything needs a cause." It is "everything that BEGINS to exist needs a cause." God did not begin; He is the Necessary Being. So the question is a category mistake.
- An endless chain of creators (infinite regress) is impossible; the chain must stop at an uncaused First.
- The Prophet ﷺ told us this doubt comes from Shaytan: "Satan comes to one of you and says, 'Who created so-and-so?' till he says, 'Who created your Lord?' When he does that, let him seek refuge with Allah and stop." — Sahih al-Bukhari 3276, Sahih Muslim 134 (agreed upon; verified on sunnah.com). (A separate narration adds saying "Amantu billah" — I believe in Allah.)

**Sources:**
- https://islamqa.info/en/answers/6660
- https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3276
- https://sunnah.com/muslim:134

**Notes:** STRONG and simple. Just fix the false principle ("begins to exist").

## OBJ-7: I Can Be Good Without God
**id:** obj-good-without-god | **difficulty:** Easy

**Steelman:** Atheists live moral lives, give to charity, love their families. So we don't need God to be good.

**Islamic response:**
- Two different questions: (a) CAN an atheist ACT good? Yes, of course. (b) Can atheism JUSTIFY that some things are REALLY, objectively wrong? That's the hard part.
- Without God, "good" tends to become just opinion, feelings, or what society agrees on — which can change. See ARG-5 and Mackie's admission.

**Notes:** Never say atheists are bad people. That's a strawman and rude. Separate "living good" from "grounding good."

## OBJ-8: Science Explains Everything / Naturalism
**id:** obj-science | **difficulty:** Medium

**Steelman:** Science keeps replacing "God did it" with natural explanations. Give it time and it will explain the rest. Only what science can test is real (naturalism).

**Islamic response:**
- Science explains HOW things work inside nature; it does not answer WHY there is anything at all, or ground morality, meaning, or consciousness (see ARG-6).
- "Only science gives truth" is itself NOT a scientific claim — it's a philosophy claim, so it defeats itself (self-refuting).
- Islam loves science; the Quran invites us to study nature (Quran 3:190–191). No conflict between studying creation and believing in the Creator.

**Notes:** STRONG. WEAK version to avoid: attacking evolution with bad science, or claiming "scientific miracles in the Quran" as knockout proof (over-claiming — many scholars warn against this).

## OBJ-9: Consciousness Is Just a Gap (God of the Gaps)
**id:** obj-gaps | **difficulty:** Hard

**Steelman:** Every time we didn't understand something (lightning, disease) people said "God." Then science explained it. Consciousness is just the next gap.

**Islamic response:** The hard problem is argued to be different in KIND, not just an unfilled gap — you can't derive first-person feeling from third-person physics even in principle (see ARG-6). Present as strong evidence, not certainty.

## OBJ-10: Fine-Tuning Is Explained by the Multiverse
**id:** obj-multiverse | **difficulty:** Medium

**Steelman:** With enough universes, one will look fine-tuned by luck. No designer needed.

**Islamic response:** The multiverse is unobserved and adds huge unproven machinery to avoid God. A universe-maker would itself need setup. Design remains simpler. (See ARG-7.)

**Sources:** https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/fine-tuning/

## OBJ-11: Kalam Objections (Quantum / Eternal Universe)
**id:** obj-kalam | **who:** Graham Oppy, physicists | **difficulty:** Hard

**Steelman:** Premise 1 (whatever begins has a cause) is from inside the universe; quantum particles seem to appear without a clear cause; and some models have no beginning.

**Islamic response:** Quantum events happen in a quantum vacuum (not "nothing") under laws — not truly uncaused from nothing. A completed infinite past is philosophically problematic. Be honest that the physics is debated; lean on the philosophy. (See ARG-2.)

## OBJ-12: Qadr vs Free Will
**id:** obj-qadr | **difficulty:** Hard

**Steelman:** If Allah already decreed and knows everything I'll do, I'm not really free — so punishing me is unfair.

**Islamic response:**
- Islam affirms BOTH: Allah's decree AND real human choice. Allah's knowledge does not FORCE you; He knows what you will freely choose.
- We feel and use real choice daily; we distinguish voluntary acts (moving your hand) from involuntary (heartbeat).
- Extremes rejected: Jabarites (no free will) and Qadarites (God has no control) are both wrong. Mainstream = the middle (Ash'ari "kasb"/acquisition).

**Sources:**
- https://islamqa.info/en/answers/264354
- https://yaqeeninstitute.org/read/paper/predestination-vs-free-will-in-islam-understanding-allahs-qadr

**Notes:** Say honestly: this is subtle and even great scholars call it hard for the mind to fully grasp.

## OBJ-13: Hell and Justice
**id:** obj-hell | **difficulty:** Medium

**Steelman:** Eternal hell for temporary, finite sins is disproportionate and cruel.

**Islamic response:**
- Punishment matches the person's state and their rejection, not just clock-time of the act. Someone who would reject truth forever is judged on that.
- Allah's mercy is vast; He forgives all sins for those who turn to Him; no one enters hell unjustly.
- Scholars differ on details of eternity for some; keep to mainstream and flag differences.

**Notes:** Handle gently, especially with someone in pain.

## OBJ-14: Why Islam and Not Another Religion?
**id:** obj-why-islam | **difficulty:** Medium

**Steelman:** Even if a god exists, why YOUR religion? You believe Islam mostly because you were born in a Muslim family (religious diversity / geography objection).

**Islamic response:**
- Move from "a God exists" to "which message is true" using evidence: the Quran (its preservation, challenge, and content), the truthfulness and life of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, and pure monotderm (tawhid) that fits the fitrah.
- The "born into it" point cuts both ways: an atheist born in a secular society could be an atheist by environment too. Origin of a belief doesn't decide its truth (genetic fallacy).

**Notes:** This needs its own evidence (Quran + prophethood). Be ready to shift the topic here honestly.

## OBJ-15: Hume on Miracles
**id:** obj-miracles | **who:** David Hume | **difficulty:** Hard

**Steelman:** Hume (*Enquiry*, "Of Miracles"): a miracle breaks the laws of nature, which have the strongest possible evidence. It's always more likely the witness lied or was fooled than that a law broke. So never believe a miracle on testimony.

**Islamic response:**
- Hume's rule can be too strong: it would make us reject any truly new event. Probability should follow the quality and quantity of evidence, not rule miracles out in advance.
- The Quran's main "sign" is not a one-off physical trick but a lasting, examinable text (its challenge to produce the like of it).

**Sources:** https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/miracles/

## OBJ-16: Meaning of Life / Nihilism
**id:** obj-nihilism | **who:** Camus, Sartre, Nietzsche | **difficulty:** Medium

**Steelman:** Without God, life has no built-in meaning (nihilism). Existentialists (Sartre, Camus) say we must invent our own meaning and face the "absurd."

**Islamic response:**
- Made-up meaning is fragile; if we invent it, we can un-invent it, and it isn't really objective.
- Islam gives real, given purpose: to know, love, and worship God (Quran 51:56), with dignity, accountability, and eternal hope.
- Notice how much effort atheist thinkers spend TRYING to rebuild meaning — that hunger itself is a sign (fitrah).

**Notes:** Great for the "existentialist/nihilist" persona in file 06.

## OBJ-17: Common Ex-Muslim Objections
**id:** obj-exmuslim | **difficulty:** Mixed

**Steelman (say kindly):** Often a MIX of intellectual doubts (science, evil, hell, women's rulings, hadith reliability) AND pain (bad community, harsh family, trauma, feeling judged). Sometimes politics or bad experiences, not pure philosophy.

**Islamic response:**
- FIRST find the real root: intellectual doubt vs bad experience vs no exposure. Ask, don't lecture.
- For pain: listen, validate feelings, separate "bad Muslims" from Islam itself.
- For intellect: give sources, admit what you don't know, connect them with knowledgeable scholars.
- Protect your own heart; some debates are traps. Kindness first (Quran 41:34 — repel evil with what is better).

**Notes:** This is the most SENSITIVE persona. Safety and gentleness over "winning."
